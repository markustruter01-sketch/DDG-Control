## 1. The answer in one minute

1. **Your real asset is not coffee, trading or fitness. It is the machine that built them.** One person with AI staff shipped a store, a trading platform, a fitness app and a control room in months. Almost nobody can do that.
2. **Sell the machine first.** DDG Build is the only part that can bring in thousands of dollars per sale right now. One mid-size Build job is worth more than all coffee sales to date.
3. **Turn every Build job into a kit with a monthly fee.** One-off jobs pay the bills. Monthly fees are what make a company valuable.
4. **Your own businesses become the showroom.** Coffee, Finance and Hypest prove the kits work. Run them lean and let them grow through other people's audiences.
5. **Tell the story every week.** "One person, AI staff, five businesses, every number public" is the cheapest marketing you will ever get.
6. **Test before you build.** Especially Hypest. Most fitness apps earn almost nothing.

If I had to put it in one line: **Build pays, kits compound, the brands prove it, the story sells it, Finance waits its turn.**

## 3. Why Build first, and why kits

**Money per sale.** A website is from $1,500. A store is from $7,500. A platform is from $11,250 (FACT, your price list). All coffee sales so far are $1,791.60 (FACT).

**Why monthly fees matter more than big jobs.** When small online businesses are sold, the price depends on the type:

| Type | Typical sale price | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Software with monthly fees | 3 to 5 times one year of that income | FACT, 2026 market guides |
| Online store | 2 to 3 times yearly owner profit | FACT |
| Agency or service work | 1 to 2 times yearly owner profit | FACT |

Listing data says the same thing more bluntly: agencies and stores change hands at about one year of sales (FACT). **So a dollar of monthly software income is worth several dollars of one-off work.** That is the whole reason for kits.

**The three kits.** Each one is something you already built for yourself.

| Kit | What the client gets | Built from | Who buys it | Price idea (GUESS) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Shop** | Online store with card payments, subscriptions, postage rules, sales, admin screen | Cattle Dog Coffee | Small makers: roasters, bakers, pet treats, candles, market stallholders | $2,500 to $4,000 set-up + $99 to $149 a month |
| **2. Control room** | One private screen: sales, socials, to-dos, a daily AI summary | DDG Control | Any owner with more than one thing to watch | $1,500 set-up + $79 a month |
| **3. Front desk** | An AI helper that answers enquiries, books, follows up | Your bot team | Trades, clinics, salons | $1,500 set-up + $199 a month |

- Kit 3's price is in line with the market: Australian AI receptionist plans with minutes included run $149 to $299 a month, and custom ones carry a $2,000 to $5,000 set-up fee (FACT, vendor price guides).
- Custom work stays on the menu at today's prices. Kits are the fast, fixed, cheaper door in.
- **Rule: do not build a kit before a client pays for it.** Client 1 pays for the work. Client 2 pays for turning it into a kit. Client 3 onward is nearly all profit.

**How to get clients — Markus's rules (17 Nov)**
- **No talking.** No calls, no meetings, no walking in. Everything in writing.
- **Nobody Markus knows.** No friends, family, suppliers, customers or business contacts. New people only.
- **Only people who have shown they want a build.** No blanket emails.
- **Prices:** listed prices stay (already about 25% below typical). First five clients get the founding price, 15% below listed (Markus 19 Nov; was 13%): website $1,275, store $6,375, platform or app $9,560, AI assistant $3,185.

**The channels, best chance first**

| # | Channel | Why it fits | Honest catch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Upwork** | Buyers post a job and a budget, so the need is proven. Proposals are written. Each one carries a small mock-up. | A new profile has no reviews, so the first wins are slow. Some buyers insist on a call; skip those. |
| 2 | **Australian job boards** where people post "I need a website" (INFERRED: Airtasker, Freelancer, Oneflare, ServiceSeeking; Opus confirms which still take web jobs and what they charge) | Same proven need, less competition from overseas | Smaller jobs |
| 3 | **Buy-it-now kits** on ddg.limited: fixed price, written brief form, deposit by card, no conversation | Fits "no talking" perfectly | Needs visitors; they come from 4 and 6 |
| 4 | **Fiverr and similar: the kits as fixed listings** | Buyers come to you and order in writing | Price pressure; kits only, never custom work |
| 5 | **Mock-up emails, only on a signal** | Our edge: a mock-up costs us little | Only worth it with a strict filter (below) |
| 6 | **Markus's own content**, each post with one ask and the quote link | Free, already happening | Slow |

**The signal filter for mock-up emails (channel 5).** A business gets a mock-up only if it shows at least two of these in public:
1. Its own site says "coming soon", is broken, or shows a security warning.
2. It is paying for ads that land on a poor page.
3. It has asked in public for a web builder (a post, a job ad, a tender).
4. It takes bookings or orders by phone or message only, and has many recent reviews (so it has money and customers).
5. It registered or opened in the last 90 days.

One email, one follow-up, then stop. Each email is personal, says who DDG is, and offers a way to opt out. Check the wording with the lawyer.

**Return on effort: measure, then cut.**
- For each channel, count: sent, replies, paid jobs, AI cost, Markus's minutes.
- **Stop rule:** a channel with no paid job after 40 tries is paused and the effort moves up the list.
- Guess to steer by: 10 written proposals a week on channels 1 and 2.
- Writing-only costs some big jobs, since larger buyers like a call. Kits and small fixed jobs suit it best.
- Never run more than two builds at once.

**Before the first paid job:** lawyer contract, professional indemnity insurance, and a GST decision (section 8).

## 4. Cattle Dog Coffee — grow through other people's audiences

A new coffee brand cannot out-spend the big ones on ads. It can borrow audiences.

1. **Christmas, now.** Dispatch Mondays left this year: 23 Nov, 30 Nov, 7 Dec, 14 Dec, 21 Dec. Push "send coffee as a gift" and pre-paid 3 or 6 month gift subscriptions. Pre-paid gifts are cash up front.
2. **Rescue fundraiser links.** Every dog rescue and breed club gets its own link. When a supporter buys through it, that group earns per bag, tracked live. You already built the per-bag counter.
   - This model is proven. A Brisbane roaster pays groups $10 per 1 kg bag through its "bean drive", and a Canadian one pays $4 a bag, fully online with no minimum (FACT).
   - Your edge: animal groups only, fully automatic, public live totals.
   - Needs a short written agreement with each group and a check of fundraising rules (see the audit).
3. **The charity partnership, once signed.** A co-branded bag and one mention in a big charity's newsletter could beat a year of your own posts. Pay in $100 lots as they asked, and show "Raised" and "Paid" on the site.
4. **Offices, cafes, vets, groomers.** A standing monthly order of 1 kg bags. One office equals many home customers and never forgets to reorder.
5. **A card in every parcel.** One QR code: a free week of Hypest, chapter one of the novel, and "give a friend $5 off, get $5 off". Cost: a few cents.
6. **Subscriptions over one-off bags, always.** Every screen should nudge to a plan.

**First job before any of this:** know the real profit per bag and per plan. DDG Control already estimates coffee profit (FACT); the job is to show it per bag and per plan, and add your packing time. If a bag makes $4, we need volume. If it makes $12, we can afford to pay rescues more. Sales per bag so far average about $29 including postage ($1,791.60 ÷ 61; FACT, rough).

**When packing takes more than four hours a week,** ask Coffee Alliance about posting orders for you, or use a packing service. Your hours are worth more on Build.

## 5. Hypest — prove people want it before building more

**The hard truth (FACT):**
- Only 5% of health and fitness apps reach US$10,000 in total sales in their first two years.
- Health and fitness has the worst first-renewal rate of any app category: about 30% stay.
- It is also the only category where yearly plans beat monthly ones (about 61% of income).
- Longer trials convert better: 17 to 32 day trials convert about 43% against about 26% for trials of four days or less.

**What that means for Hypest**
1. **Stop building features. Start testing demand.** No watches, no phone-app wrapper, until strangers pay.
2. **Pick one sharp group, not "everyone who wants to get fit".** Test three with simple "coming soon" pages and a waitlist:
   - **Pass your service fitness test** (defence, police, fire, ambulance). You are ex-RAAF; you can speak to this. Be honest about the competition: Defence and Victoria Police already give recruits their own free apps (FACT). Hypest must offer what those do not: a coach that adapts every week, every service in one place.
   - **Train for a date**: first 10 km, first obstacle race, first fitness event. A deadline makes people pay.
   - **Shift workers**: 20-minute plans that fit rosters.
3. **Whichever page gets the most sign-ups per 100 visitors wins.** Launch only that.
4. **Add a yearly plan** and keep the US$2.50 first plan as a paid trial. Your four-week first plan is already the right length.
5. **Cap the AI cost** at 15% of what a user pays (GUESS). Measure it per user from day one.
6. **A written keep-or-pause rule.** 90 days after launch: if fewer than 100 people are paying (GUESS), or fewer than 30% renew, pause consumer marketing.
7. **The fallback is valuable.** Sell Hypest to personal trainers and gyms as "your own AI coach app" through DDG Build. Businesses cancel far less than consumers.

Lawyer review and insurance come before live payments (see the audit).
